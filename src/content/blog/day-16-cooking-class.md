# Quick Morning
We started off the morning with a tragic mistake: we thought we needed breakfast. Sharyq went to hunt and gather us a croissant before our cooking class pickup at 8:30 while I heroically slept in. We finished our coffees and croissants and went downstairs to pet the cats while we waited for our pickup. 

# The Start of Class

The pickup came and told us the plan for our day: we were to start at the morning market, where we would see the ingredients we’d be using, then we’d get driven to the cooking school where we would assemble 11 dishes. 

The morning market was quite the place to be, and our subgroup of 7 (out of 14) generated a bit of a traffic jam at the stalls we visited. We went to the vegetable stalls, the tofu stalls, the dry goods stalls, interspersed with some breakfast snacks (this is when we learned we had made a mistake eating those croissants). We had a few sweet snacks, including banana steamed in sticky rice, and a few savory snacks, such as the northern style sausage with lemongrass. My favorite was a snack where you took fermented tea leaves and put some toasted coconut, ginger, and sugar in them as a sort of very small wrap. It was a fun combination of sweet, sour, and fermented savoriness. 

At the cooking school we started by cooking papaya salad and spring rolls. It was fun to learn how the Thais used the massive ceramic pestle and mortars to get so much flavor out of their herbs for the salad and mash the papaya a little bit to let all the flavors seep in. The spring rolls were not very novel, but each time someone fried their spring roll, the teacher made them sing a song ‘one elephant, two elephant, three elephant, …’ to keep track of how long it had been frying (I cooked mine for 7 elephants). We were also shown how to make our first desert: pumpkin in coconut milk. It was pretty self explanatory. 

We next learned how to make a curry, winter melon soup, and a cucumber salad. The winter melon soup and cucumber salad were both dishes from the Akha hill tribe, members of which were running this cooking school. The Akha people immigrated from Tibet into Myanmar, then eventually Thailand, in the early 19th century, a legacy that was very clearly shown in the winter melon soup and cucumber salad, since Thai’s don’t really use either ingredient, especially winter melon. We cooked the soup by simply boiling winter melon cubes and lemongrass, with a bit of mushroom stock added, and the cucumber salad was just chopped cucumbers, tomatoes, peanuts, and lime juice. The curry took a lot more effort. I decided to make the green curry and Sharyq had decided to make the massaman curry. We were given bit granite mortar and pestles and were told to ‘be angry’ with it. Since I had made my papaya salad quite spicy, I chose to tone down the chilis for my curry, but Sharyq decided to throw wayyyy too many chilis into the pestle, because his curry was almost inedible (he insists it was ‘really good’ and just ‘had a little kick to it’). We then transferred this paste, which I had managed to get very fine, into the pot the cook with coconut milk, vegetables, and chicken to make our curry. 

# Post Curry

After eating all that, we couldn’t even imagine eating more, yet we still had 4 dishes left. We learned how Thais steam sticky rice to make mango sticky rice, and we were shown how to make an Akha spicy dip, made out of chilis, coriander, tomatoes, garlic and peanuts, and we ate it with sticky rice and banana flower. We made some tom yum soup, which I discovered uses the key ingredient of a special chili sauce that I need to buy before I leave Thailand, and we ended off all our dishes with a stir fry. Sharyq decided to make Pad Thai and I decided to make hot basil chicken. There weren’t many surprises there, but someone had burnt their chilis (we think it was this one English kid) and had fumigated the entire room with cough-inducing smoke. We ate all of our dishes and finished up our class!

# We were super full

After leaving the class, Sharyq and I realized just how much food we had eaten and took a 2 hour long food coma nap. Afterwards, we didn’t quite know what to do, since we still weren’t hungry but figured we should still go to dinner, so we went to a mall called One Nimman. It was boutique mall with European architecture where we did a bit of shopping (I bought two bracelets) and got some small bites for dinner. We then continued to a different, indoor mall, walked around a bit, then returned home. We had wanted to go to some queer bar, especially since the two days left in Chiang Mai are national dry days, but we were just too stuffed and had to sleep the food off.
